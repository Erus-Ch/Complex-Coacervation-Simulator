Equation & scope

The starting point is the classical VO combination of mixing entropy and Debye–Hückel correlations. The χ term is a visible optional extension.

Conserved phase variables
\[\phi_P=\phi_Q=\frac{p}{2},\quad\phi_+=\phi_-=n,\quad\phi_w=1-p-2n\]
Reduced Helmholtz free-energy density
\[\tilde f=\frac{p}{N}\ln\frac p2+2n\ln n+\phi_w\ln\phi_w-\alpha(qp+2n)^{3/2}+\chi_{pw}p\phi_w\]
Debye–Hückel coefficient
\[\alpha=\frac{2\sqrt\pi}{3}\left(\frac{l_B}{a}\right)^{3/2},\quad \kappa^2=\frac{4\pi l_B}{a^3}(qp+2n)\]
Coexistence construction
\[g(p;\mu_n)=\min_n[\tilde f(p,n)-\mu_n n],\quad g'(p_\alpha)=g'(p_\beta)=\frac{g(p_\beta)-g(p_\alpha)}{p_\beta-p_\alpha}\]

Here \(\tilde f=f a^3/(k_BT)\). The ion screening sum uses charged sites \(qp\), not a whole-molecule charge squared. Both phases are locally electroneutral in this symmetric reduction. The plotted critical point is a numerical estimate from the converging binodal branches. This classical VO simulator is qualitative for concentrated solutions and for small multivalent molecules: it neglects site connectivity, finite ion size, specific ion effects, and detailed spacer chemistry. It predicts ion enrichment in the dense phase in this regime; experiments can show the opposite. Source: Sing & Perry, Soft Matter (2020), Figs. 1–3.

Asymmetric VO free energy and ion balance

Same VO mixing plus Debye–Hückel structure, evaluated on the charge-neutral composition space.

Polyion charge stoichiometry
\[R_q=\frac{q_PN_Pc_P}{q_QN_Qc_Q},\quad c_{\rm tot}=c_P+c_Q\]
Bulk electroneutrality
\[q_P\phi_P-q_Q\phi_Q-\phi_X+\phi_Y+z_+\phi_{S^+}+z_-\phi_{S^-}=0\]
VO free-energy density
\[\tilde f=\frac{\phi_P}{N_P}\ln\phi_P+\frac{\phi_Q}{N_Q}\ln\phi_Q+\sum_{i\in\{X,Y,S^+,S^-\}}\phi_i\ln\phi_i+\phi_w\ln\phi_w-\alpha I_{\rm DH}^{3/2}+\chi_{pw}(\phi_P+\phi_Q)\phi_w\]
Screening, water and salt ionic strength
\[I_{\rm DH}=q_P\phi_P+q_Q\phi_Q+\phi_X+\phi_Y+z_+^2\phi_{S^+}+z_-^2\phi_{S^-},\quad I_s=\tfrac12(\nu_+z_+^2+\nu_-z_-^2)c_s\]
Water fraction
\[\phi_w=1-\phi_P-\phi_Q-\phi_X-\phi_Y-\phi_{S^+}-\phi_{S^-}\]
Debye–Hückel scaling
\[\alpha=\frac{2\sqrt\pi}{3}\left(\frac{l_B}{a}\right)^{3/2},\quad\kappa^2=\frac{4\pi l_B}{a^3}I\]
Initial ion inventory
\[c_{X^-}^{0}=q_P c_P^0,\quad c_{Y^+}^{0}=q_Q c_Q^0,\quad c_-^{0}=c_{X^-}^{0}+c_s,\quad c_+^{0}=c_{Y^+}^{0}+c_s\]

This selectable-salt extension uses monovalent polyion sites and one monovalent counterion per charged site; salt ions follow the selected formula stoichiometry and are explicit ideal-mixing species. Salt identity enters through ion count, valence, electroneutrality, and Debye–Hückel screening. Ion-specific activity coefficients, binding, and finite ion sizes are not included. The 3D z-coordinate is salt formula-unit concentration cs; Is is shown separately.

Reading the high-dimensional result

Each fixed-Rq plane is a total-polyion-molarity versus selected-salt cross-section.

Only the spinodal surface is shown here: it marks local loss of stability computed from the smallest eigenvalue of the constrained free-energy Hessian. The global binodal is a separate common-tangent surface, so the spinodal should not be interpreted as the full two-phase coexistence boundary. The salt formula concentration is cs, while its ionic strength is Is = ½(ν+z+²+ν−z−²)cs. Local electroneutrality is enforced in each bulk phase; interfacial double layers are not part of this bulk VO model.
